## Why it works
1. **Three clear roles:** a flawed human (lazy, a bit shameless), a careful AI (asks, worries), a doer AI (one word). The contrast between the two AIs is the comedy engine.
2. **Rule of three:** the same gag three times, faster each time, then broken by the twist.
3. **A fourth character who never appears.** The client exists only as notifications. Zero modelling cost, real stakes.
4. **The tool is revealed by a question** ("how?") after we've already seen what it does three times. Show, then name.
5. **The twist is about the human, not the tool** (he bills like an agency). The last word is the gag's catchphrase.
6. The AI companion sits at the human's **eye level** (on the shoulder), so both fit in one close-up.

## What we take
- An off-screen recurring character via notifications.
- Rule of three with a catchphrase.
- "Show three times, then name it."
- Put the small character at the human's eye level.

## What we don't take
- Mascots that are another company's product personified (our rule: no character resembling a company's logo or mascot).
- Their characters, set, brand-style thumbnails or lines.
- The claim in the caption (agency price vs free) without a source.
