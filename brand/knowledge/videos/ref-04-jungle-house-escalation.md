## What it is
A face-cam creator shows an AI-generated explainer ("you are lost in the jungle, build a house that survives") and then reveals the tool that generated it. The explainer is the lesson for us; the pitch is the lesson about the tool.

## Look
- Retro "early-2000s life-sim game" 3D: simple characters, flat textures, bright daylight.
- Creator's face-cam in a corner the whole time.
- **One-word captions** in the middle of the frame, popping word by word (narration, not dialogue).
- Big labels on the key facts only: the deadliest animal, a percentage, the name of the gadget, a height.

## Why it holds attention
1. **Second person.** "You" are the protagonist from the first second. No character needs introducing.
2. **Escalation loop:** problem → fix → "But…" → new problem, about every 8 s. Every fix is visible (the house physically changes), so the viewer can follow with the sound off.
3. **One persistent object that transforms** (the house). Same principle as ref 2's map and ref 3's whiteboard.
4. **A surprising, checkable fact** every second loop, with a big label.
5. **A final test** proves the design (days of rain) before the congratulation.
6. The reveal ("you watched the whole thing") turns the viewer's own attention into the proof for the pitch.

## What we take
- The escalation loop as a narrative mode: **"You are X. Problem. Fix. But…"** Good for "how does this attack / protocol / chip work" topics.
- One object that visibly changes with each fix.
- Labels only on the 3–4 facts worth remembering.

## What we don't take
- The game look, the house scenario and its facts, the face-cam layout.
- The tool (see `../tools.md` → Higgsfield): paid, and it generates from someone else's viral video as a template. Not a fit for our brand rules.
- Unsourced numbers. Their percentage and "deadliest animal" labels have no source on screen or in the caption.
