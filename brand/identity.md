# Brand identity: Under the Prompt / Debajo del Prompt

Status: **approved 2026-11-05: the user chose Mott.** Once approved, this file and `brand/mott.md` are the permanent foundation for every video. `brand/guidelines.md` (tone, CTAs) stays valid. Its duration (60–90 s) and colour sections get replaced by this file after approval.

The governing rule is **consistency over novelty**: someone should recognize a video as ours within two seconds, without seeing the handle.

(55.6 s), "ELI5 #4: What's MCP?" (68.4 s) and "Daily Life: why not use AI?" (35.8 s). I also checked the profile grid: 30 posts, 73.5K followers.)
- **No narrator. Two characters talk.** A human developer (the protagonist) and an AI sidekick. The **captions are the dialogue**: one short sentence at a time, white text in a dark translucent rounded box in the upper third.
- **A recurring human protagonist does the actions.** The AI characters react and help. The story is hers.
- **Very few shot types, one home set:** a close-up of her, a close-up of the sidekick, and a medium shot of both with the whiteboard. A city at night in bokeh, shallow depth of field.
- **The ELI5 formula** (identical in #1 and #4):
  1. She asks: "Claude. Explain X like I'm five."
  2. The AI answers in jargon ("an interface that lets two systems…"), and the jargon is **crossed out in red** on the whiteboard.
  3. She pushes back: "I'm five."
  4. The AI draws **one analogy, built up step by step on a persistent whiteboard** (API = you / waiter / kitchen; MCP = phone + one cable + plugs), pointing at it with a stick.
  5. **Labels map the analogy onto the real terms** ("= front end", "= API", "= back end").
  6. A one-line recap.
  7. A **punchline callback** at the end ("That's a bug." / "Too soon, Claude.").
- **Daily Life formula:** a relatable work situation (the client asks "why not use AI? make it faster") with a reaction shot after every line and a twist at the end ("Good news. We already use AI. That's why it's three months.").
- **Almost no on-screen text besides the dialogue and the whiteboard.** No data cards and no sources in the frame.
- **Named series with episode numbers on every cover** ("EXPLAIN IT LIKE I'M FIVE · PART 5", "LET'S TRY THIS · EP 1", "DAILY LIFE · EP 17"). The cover is a 2–3 word line with the key word in colour.
- **What this means for us:**
  - People perform the story and Mott narrates, explains and reacts.
  - Explanations use **one persistent drawn analogy that builds up**, with jargon crossed out.
  - Minimal on-screen text and a punchline at the end.
  - A series label and episode number on every video.

**What we keep (the grammar):**
- stylized low-poly 3D lit by one motivated warm light;
- characters that *react* and drive the story;
- UI cards as evidence, colour-coded by meaning and with the source shown;
- one persistent world that transforms inside an episode;
- a series format with a visible progress marker;
- short shots with steady, simple camera moves;
- karaoke captions.

**What we must not copy:**
- the AI mascot in ref 1 (an orange starburst tied to a real company's brand), its orange-on-navy palette, the human developer's design, the map style and green accent of ref 2, and either video's layouts or text;
- generally: no character that resembles any company's logo or mascot.

## 2. Three character concepts

### A. MOTT, the night-shift moth *(recommended)*
- **What:** a small, faceted low-poly moth who lives under the glow of a giant blinking cursor, the "_" of a prompt. It's the brand name made literal: Mott lives *under the prompt*.
- **Look:** a fuzzy dusk-violet body, huge glossy black eyes that always reflect an amber catchlight, and long feathery antennae. The wing eyespots carry the brand's "_" cursor mark. Folded wings give a teardrop silhouette; open wings make it wide.
- **Personality:** endlessly curious and easily dazzled by bright new tech. Mott knows exactly what happens to moths that fly too close to bright lights, so it became the internet's most careful fact-checker.
- **Humor:** self-aware moth jokes (lights, being a "bug", "I don't eat sweaters, that's my cousins"), understatement, small physical gags (a puff of wing dust when embarrassed).
- **World:** night desks, machine rooms and screens, always seen from Mott's 4 cm scale.
- **Strengths:**
  - a unique silhouette, even at thumbnail size;
  - a metaphor built in (light = hype; "bug" = computing history);
  - emotion that reads without a complex face, through antennae, wings and eyelids;
  - a name that works in English and Spanish.

### B. RAM, the veteran sysadmin ram
- **What:** a stocky low-poly ram whose wool is chunky looped cable. The curled horns are coiled cables ending in connector tips, and it wears a teal cardigan.
- **Personality:** forty years "on call", deadpan, has survived every hype cycle ("I remember when 'the cloud' was a server in a closet"). Grumpy on the outside, protective of its audience.
- **Humor:** dry one-liners and "back in my day" comparisons that end up explaining the new thing.
- **World:** a cozy server closet with blinking LEDs, mugs and cable spaghetti.
- **Palette:** oat wool `#E8DCC4`, terracotta `#C8553D`, teal `#2A7F7A`, LED green `#5BE37D`.
- **Strengths:** the natural skeptic for debunking stories. "RAM" is a pun people get in both languages.
- **Risks:**
  - the grumpy veteran is a familiar archetype;
  - excitement doesn't suit it, which is awkward for good-news stories;
  - wool and horns need more geometry, so they cost more to animate.

### C. NIMBO, the cloud that is literally "the cloud"
- **What:** a soft, chunky low-poly cloud with a face, whose weather *is* its emotion: rain when sad, lightning for a security alarm, sun rays for good news, fog when confused. It can reshape itself into diagrams, such as a server rack, an arrow or a lock.
- **Personality:** dramatic, fast-talking, an internet gossip ("I'm literally the cloud, I see everything"). A slightly unreliable narrator that corrects itself mid-sentence.
- **Humor:** melodrama and weather gags.
- **World:** a bright daytime sky over a miniature city of devices.
- **Palette:** cloud white `#F6F8FF`, sky blue `#6CB8FF`, sun `#FFC93C`, storm indigo `#4B4F8C`.
- **Strengths:**
  - emotions are huge and readable;
  - shape-shifting gives it explanatory power;
  - a light theme stands out in a dark feed.
- **Risks:**
  - a cloud is a generic shape, so the silhouette is weak;
  - shape-shifting works against visual consistency;
  - the pun narrows it toward cloud topics;
  - it's the least cinematic of the three.

## 3. Recommendation: Mott

1. **Recognizable in two seconds.** The antennae-and-wings silhouette, an amber-lit night world and the "_" eyespot make the brand readable even muted and without the handle.
2. **The brand name becomes a place.** "Under the prompt" is where Mott lives, under the cursor's light. Every opening and ending happens there, which gives the channel a home set like a TV show.
3. **A built-in editorial stance.** Moths are drawn to bright lights and get burned, and tech hype is a bright light. Mott's running conflict, *dazzled vs. careful*, is the channel's promise: curiosity plus fact-checking. It also gives every video a natural "is it a lamp or a fire?" beat.
4. **Animation that's feasible in our pipeline.**
   - Emotion lives in antennae, wings, eyelids and catchlight size.
   - These are simple procedural rotations and scales, not facial blendshapes or lip-sync rigs, so the character stays consistent because it is the same code every time.
5. **Range.** Mott can be excited (good news), scared (security), skeptical (hype) or moved (history) without breaking character. Ram can't do excitement well, and Nimbo struggles with seriousness.
6. **Room for a second voice without a second rig.** The **Cursor** (the blinking "_" lamp) is Mott's silent partner. It "answers" through typed UI cards, which turns evidence cards into dialogue, like the AI chat in ref 1 but original.

The full reusable spec is in [`mott.md`](mott.md).

## 4. Visual identity: "Lamplight"

### Colours
| Role | Name | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Background | Night Ink | `#0E0B14` | Darkness, at least 40% of every frame. Violet-black, never pure black. |
| Ambient | Shadow Plum | `#241B33` | Fill-lit surfaces, environment midtones. |
| **Primary** | Lamp Amber | `#FFB23E` | The key light, attention, the "_" mark, progress bar, active caption word. |
| Warm hot spot | Ember | `#FF7A2F` | Hot spots of light. Also "hype" and heat, used sparingly. |
| **Character** | Mott Violet | `#7A68D8` | Mott's body and wings. Reserved for Mott only. |
| Character light | Fuzz Lilac | `#B9AEF2` | Mott's fuzz, rim light, wing edges. |
| Truth | Verified Ice | `#A8E4FF` | Facts, sources, success, "verified" cards. |
| Danger | Alarm Coral | `#FF5A6E` | Errors, risks, malicious things. Only while the problem exists. |
| Text | Cream | `#FFF1D6` | All text on dark. |

Rules:
- Amber means *look here*.
- Ice means *this is verified*.
- Coral means *problem*.
- Violet means *Mott*.
- No other saturated hue appears unless the topic needs a real brand colour, such as a product's own logo in a real capture.

### Lighting principles
1. **One motivated warm key per shot.** It always comes from a visible source: the cursor-lamp, a screen, a relay lamp. What matters in the story is what's lit.
2. **Cool violet fill and lilac rim** separate Mott from the dark.
3. **Atmosphere:** light haze plus a few slow **dust motes floating in the light beam**, our signature texture. Bloom only on light sources, never on everything.
4. **Shallow depth of field** on close-ups, deeper focus on wide shots.
5. **Darkness is the frame.** No flat, evenly lit shots.

### Materials and textures
- **Low-poly, flat-shaded** (faceted normals), matte (roughness ≥ 0.8), no photo textures.
- Mott's fuzz is a slightly jittered faceted shell with a lilac rim.
- **Screens and UI are emissive**, with very faint scanlines (a nod to the Fósforo look the user chose earlier).
- No chrome, glass or hologram surfaces.
- Real product captures are shown *on a screen inside the world*, labeled as real. Schematic recreations are labeled "schematic" or "recreación".

### Character proportions
Mott stands 1.0 unit tall: head 0.45, thorax 0.35, abdomen 0.25 (overlapping). Antennae add 0.55. Wingspan open is 1.6. In-world scale: **Mott is 4 cm**, so everything else is giant. Scale is how we show magnitudes. Full measurements are in `mott.md`.

### Camera language
- **Three shot sizes:** close-up (Mott's face fills ~60% of the width, for emotion), medium (Mott plus the object it interacts with) and wide (the world, at scale).
- **One move per shot:**
  - a slow push-in for revelations;
  - a 10–15° orbit for thinking;
  - a crane-up to reveal scale;
  - the **flutter-cam**, a soft handheld wobble while following Mott in flight, as the signature move.
- A shot changes every **2–4 s** (ref 1 averages 2.7 s), but camera motion is continuous inside an episode's world (ref 2).
- Shots are framed for 9:16 safe zones: nothing important above y 220 or below y 1500, or in the right 140 px.

### Environment design
- **Home set "The Lamp":** a night desk seen at Mott's scale. Giant keycaps form the terrain, a monitor is a wall of light, a window shows a city in bokeh, and **a giant blinking "_" cursor hangs overhead as the lamp**.
  - Every episode opens and closes here.
  - Moth-scale props: a coffee mug (a tower), a cable (a road), a sticky-note stack (a stair).
- **Topic sets** come from one modular kit with the same light rules and materials: Server Aisle, Vintage Machine Room, Street of Phones, Data Field (a floor of tiles or hexagons that can extrude, inspired by ref 2's 3D moment), Archive (logbooks, folders).
- One persistent set per episode, which transforms instead of being replaced.

### Typography
- **Inter** 800 for kinetic titles and captions. **JetBrains Mono** 500/700 for UI cards, code, IDs and numbers. Both are already loaded (SIL OFL).
- Kinetic titles: at most 4 words, Cream, with the key word in Amber.
- The series stamp "**NIGHT LOG #001**" (ES "**BITÁCORA #001**") is set in JetBrains Mono.

### Subtitle design
- A **karaoke pill**: a rounded pill in `rgba(14,11,20,.86)` with 18 px padding.
- Inter 800 at 56 px, Cream text, the **active word in Lamp Amber**.
- At most 4 words or 24 characters per chunk, breaking on punctuation.
- Fixed position: centre at y ≈ 1390, above the platforms' UI, which keeps it consistent and readable on any background.
- Speaker colour: Mott's lines plain. The Cursor's typed replies appear as cards, never as subtitles.

### UI cards (evidence)
- A dark panel in `#120D18` at 92% with an 18 px radius and a 2 px border colour-coded Amber (info), Ice (verified/result) or Coral (danger).
- A small header tab with the Cursor mark "▍_", mono numbers, and a **`source:` line on every number**.
- Cards appear *in the world* (on a screen or floating at Mott's height, lit by it), or full-screen for at most 3 s.

### Transition language (only these three)
1. **Blink cut:** the cursor-lamp blinks off and on, and the cut happens in the dark frame. It's the signature transition, used at most twice per video.
2. **Fly-through:** Mott flies into a light (a screen, a lamp) and comes out in the next set.
3. **Hard cut on a word** for everything else.
- No wipes, spins, glitches or zoom-blurs.

### Sound design
- **Voice** sits at the centre, around −16 LUFS short-term, with the master at −14 LUFS integrated (matching both references).
- **Music bed:**
  - an original ambient pad in a minor-sixth colour, with a soft pulse at around 90 BPM;
  - it sits ~18 dB under the voice and ducks on every line;
  - **made by us** (procedurally generated, so no licence issues), or CC0 with the licence saved next to the video.
- **Signature sting, "lamp tap":** a glass *tink* plus a short low hum swell. It plays at the start (Mott taps the lamp) and at the end.
- **Mott's sounds:** a soft wing flutter (filtered noise bursts) and a tiny "hm" chirp when curious, at most 4 times per video.
- **UI:** a soft wooden click when a card appears, a tick when numbers count. At most 3 whooshes per video, and none on plain cuts.

### Series format (from ref 2's structure, made our own)
- Every video is an entry in **Mott's Night Log**:
  - a stamp "NIGHT LOG #014" in the hook;
  - a thin **amber progress bar** at the top that fills with the video;
  - "Step" pills when the story has steps.
- **Opening ritual (≤ 2 s):** the lamp blinks and Mott delivers the hook.
- **Ending ritual (≤ 3 s):** Mott says the sign-off, taps the lamp, *tink*, and the screen blinks to black. Viewers learn this beat and recognize it.

## 5. Narrative format (decided 2026-11-05)
**Mott tells the story to the intern** (`brand/host.md`), a systems student who is curious about tech.
- **The Desk** (the frame of every video): Mott and the intern at the night desk. Mott narrates; the intern asks the audience's questions and reacts.
- **The Story** (most of the screen time): the video cuts away to the story itself, where **the people in it perform the actions** (engineers searching relays, someone taping the moth into a logbook, visitors at a museum). Mott's voice continues over these shots, and Mott appears only occasionally, as a small observer.
- **Analogies** are drawn as **one persistent diagram that builds up** (whiteboard or logbook), with jargon crossed out (from ref 3).
- **Minimal on-screen text:** no sources inside frames (they go in the description), at most one short card per idea, a punchline at the end.
- **A series label and episode number** on every video and cover.

## 6. Story structure (every video, 45–75 s)

| Beat | Time | Job | Mott's role |
|---|---|---|---|
| HOOK | 0–3 s | A true, surprising statement or question. | Close-up, a reaction, the lamp blinks. |
| CONTEXT | 3–12 s | What happened and why you should care. | Flies into the topic set. |
| DEVELOPMENT | 12–45 s | The story, with visual evidence and demos. | Interacts with objects and cards, reacts. |
| "LAMP OR FIRE?" | inside development | The honesty beat: what's hype, what's true, what it can't do. | The skeptic pose. |
| PAYOFF | 45–60 s | The most interesting insight or twist. | A push-in to a close-up. |
| ENDING | last 3–5 s | A memorable line, then the sign-off ritual. | The lamp tap. |

The rules from `guidelines.md` still apply: every claim has a primary source, there are no fake UIs, and on-screen text is at most 4 words.
