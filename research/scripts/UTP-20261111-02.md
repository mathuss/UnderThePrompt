# Who can read what Gemini pulls from your Gmail / Quién puede leer lo que Gemini saca de tu Gmail

## Why now (evergreen)
Gemini's Connected Apps (Gmail, Drive, Docs, Calendar, Photos and more) are on millions of personal accounts, and Google's own help page says a subset of that data, including excerpts of emails and files, goes to human reviewers. Two Hacker News threads put it in front of a lot of people this week: "Gemini trains on chat history, even for subscribers" (737 points, 2026-11-04) and "Google Gemini routes personal Drive files and emails to human annotators" (733 points, 2026-11-09). Google's Privacy Hub was last updated 2026-10-26. Not new policy, but most users have never read it, and the fix is two settings.

## Notes for the Producer
- Every claim comes from Google's own help pages. Don't use the GitHub write-up's claims (background "autonomous" retrieval, failures of scrubbing, "Google reversed its commitment"): they're one user's informal tests.
- Don't say "Google trains on your Gmail". Google's page says it does NOT train directly on your inbox, but summaries/excerpts/inferences from it may be used. The VO says only what reviewers may see.
- Paid plans: the HN thread says "even for subscribers". Google's pages don't list an exception for paid plans, but don't claim either way in VO. Work/school (Workspace) accounts have separate terms: not covered here; don't generalize to them.
- "Adults" in EN VO = Google's "18 or over". Keep it.
- UI labels: the help page uses "Settings & help → Activity". Capture the real UI on production day; labels may differ by language. ES VO says "Keep Activity, o guardar actividad": check the actual Spanish UI label and adjust.
- The settings demo needs a signed-in Google account (user decision: own account with blurring, or a test account). Help pages can be captured without login.
- Evergreen lane: not a new policy. The VO doesn't say "new" or "this week".
