# The tweet nobody edited / El tuit que nadie editó

## Why now
On 2026-11-15, Bitly links inside 2011 tweets from Burger King and McDonald's began redirecting to unrelated Wikipedia pages, without anyone touching the accounts. Bitly confirmed the same day that "bad actors" had repurposed "certain old, inactive links" and started disabling them. It hit CNN and 331 points on Hacker News, and it's the perfect excuse to explain what a short link is, plus a free trick every viewer can use today.

## Notes for the Producer
- **Don't show the destinations** (a list of Nazi camps and the 9/11 article, per Ransomnews). Say "unrelated Wikipedia pages" only. Platforms may flag the video otherwise.
- **Don't claim the mechanism.** Bitly hasn't said how the links were taken. The table explanation is how any short link works (shown live with curl), not a reconstruction of the attack. Keep the schematic labeled.
- Bitly's statement is on X (fetch tools: HTTP 402). Quote it only as "Bitly says…"; the exact words are confirmed by two outlets (Ransomnews, MediaNama). Re-check on production day whether Bitly published a fuller write-up (blog or help center) and whether more links were affected.
- Trump's account was also affected (Ransomnews checked 97 of his links: 7 unrelated, 1 blocked). Left out on purpose to keep it non-political and one idea; the user may add it.
- The "+" info page is Bitly-only. Don't claim other shorteners have the same trick (not verified).
- The ES line "redes viejas de tu laburo" is the share prompt (playbook §8.4). EN equivalent is in the last row.
- Pronunciation: "Bitly" = "BIT-lee"; in ES too.
